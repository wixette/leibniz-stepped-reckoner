## Log

- **2026-11-24** The branch is opened from `main` after the release of 1.2.0. Release branches are named `dev-vX.Y.Z` from now on, so that they no longer share their name with the release's tag (`git` warned that `v1.2.0` was ambiguous).
- **2026-11-24** The old release branch `v1.2.0` was deleted, locally and on GitHub (it was fully merged; GitHub needed the full name `refs/heads/v1.2.0`, since the tag shares the short one). Only `main` and `dev-v1.3.0` remain.
- **2026-11-24** **Findings from the photograph of the original** (Hajotthu, CC BY 3.0, `research/images/commons/Leibniz_Rechenmaschine_(1690).jpg`), with Brandt's CAD figure and Badur's text (WALSDORF2014):
  - **The Magna Rota is a pierced, carved disc with the crank in its centre: no digits, no holes, no pin.** Our Magna Rota (holes, black and red numbers, a pin that stops the crank) follows Leupold's 1727 description, not the surviving machine. The counting is done by the revolution counter at the top right of the carriage: a ring of holes and a pin are visible there in the photo, as Badur describes. Still open for Badur: whether that pin also stops the crank.
  - **The pentagons stand up above the register, in two staggered rows,** visible from the front. Ours sit level with the register cover behind the case's back wall. There is photographic evidence now, but no measurement of their height.
  - The register of the original appears **open from above**, without a brass cover plate like ours. To check against Brandt's figure before the pentagons are raised.
- **2026-11-24** **The pierced style.** The Magna Rota becomes the original's pierced disc, Ø116.5 (BADUR2026), with the counter work (B2/B3). We draw our own scrollwork in the same style rather than tracing the photo or Brandt's model: the 1690s ornament is free, but the photo is CC BY and Brandt's model is copyrighted, and a pattern of our own keeps the triangle count modest. The pierced corner pieces on the carriage front may follow later.
- **2026-11-24** **Plan without Badur's answers** (agreed): (1) **C1, the stepped drums at the original's sizes,** first, on the dev page (its crank-angle slider and step mode) and with Blender's clearance check: the riskiest refit and independent of Badur; (2) the pierced Magna Rota; (3) a working revolution counter at the top right, the pin's job (stop or marker) a switch until Badur answers; then the pentagons' height from the photo, the crank direction as a parameter, and the large gears (C2).
- **2026-11-24** **C1 done: the stepped drums at the original's sizes** (BADUR2026): pitch circle Ø32.5 over the 16 virtual teeth (module 2.03125), tip Ø35 (addendum 0.615 m), 28.5 long, ribs 3.15 mm a digit (his drawing: 3.2, 6.3 … 25.2, 28.5, the longest 0.15 over nine steps). Not measured, so kept from Lehmann: the root (his 7/6 m dedendum, Ø27.76), the boss and the receiving gear (10 teeth, centre distance 26.5, which Badur's assembly drawing confirms). The setting pinion follows from the rib step: one dial division (360/13°) must move the drum 3.15 mm, so its module is 1.3035 (Lehmann's 1.25 gave his 3.02). Params 0.4.0; the kinematics fixture regenerated. One check caught a real weakness: the drum's square bore ended 0.01 mm from the core's end faces, which flicker at some settings (A2-14); it now ends inside the flange and the boss. All tests, Blender's clearance sweep (8631 poses) and a look at settings 1, 5 and 9 in the Stepped drum view pass.
- **2026-11-24** **Sources for the Magna Rota's pierced pattern.** Best: Klaus Badur's saw template for it ("Sägeschablone für Pos. 70", sheet 7 of his drawings, private scan): the whole disc face-on at true shape, mirror-symmetric, about a dozen cut-outs a side, the crank's hole, holes on the vertical axis and two screw holes; the neighbouring panels give its blank's dimensions. Cross-checks: the photo of the original (Hajotthu, CC BY 3.0), Brandt's CAD figure (reference only), the Dresden replica's photos (Commons, CC BY-SA). Lehmann's drawings (NL 183/465, 466 PLAN, all 136 pages looked at) have none; Leupold's engraving shows his different, numbered Magna Rota; another replica (Hannes Grobe's photo) has a different pattern.

- **2026-11-24** **The owner's drawing of the Magna Rota** (`magnarota_by_wixette.svg`, from Figma): three plates of one diameter, scaled to Ø116.5 (BADUR2026). Plate 1, the pierced brass front plate (32 cut-outs and the crank's hole), after Badur's saw template for Pos. 70 and the photo of the original; plates 2 and 3, two plain steel plates behind it (centre hole and small holes; plate 3 also three holes round the hub), after the two other discs on his sheet 7 (plate 3 is his Pos. 69: Ø116.5, three countersunk M3 holes at 120° round a hub, its holes drilled together with Pos. 70; its label looks like "Kurbelscheibe", crank disc, which would mean the Magna Rota is fixed to the crank and turns with it). The photo Leibniz-Rechenmaschine_TSD_(08) shows two silver plates behind the brass one. A hole that passes through several plates has the same diameter in each.
  - **Assumed, not confirmed:** front to back 1, 2, 3. The brass plate is certainly in front (it is the one seen); the order of the two steel plates is not shown by any source (sheet 7 lists them 69, the other, 70).
  - **Plan:** keep the SVG in the repository as the source (credited to the owner, CC BY-SA); the Blender build reads the three plate paths, flattens their curves and cuts the holes; thicknesses by eye from the photos (Badur's side view suggests about 2.5 mm for Pos. 69). Built together with the working revolution counter, since the pin and *Run to stop* live on the Magna Rota today. The owner re-exports with Figma's "Include 'id' attribute" so that each plate's path carries its layer name (`MagnaRota_Plate1` …).
- **2026-11-24** The drawing is in the repository as `design/drawings/magna-rota.svg` (the owner's own work, CC BY-SA like the other design files), re-exported from Figma with each plate's group named `MagnaRota_Plate1` … `3`. `blender/scripts/lib/svgplate.py` reads a plate by its id, flattens its curves to within 0.02 mm and scales it so that its rim is Ø116.5: plate 1 has 32 holes (the cut-outs, the crank's hole and the small round holes), plate 2 has 6, plate 3 has 9, matching the drawing shape for shape. - **2026-11-24** **The Magna Rota and the counter, as agreed for the model:** (1) the Magna Rota, the owner's three plates, turns with the crank, one turn per turn (plate 3 looks like Badur's "Kurbelscheibe", fixed to a hub); (2) the counter's steel ring, Ø69.5 / Ø45 with 10 holes, steps 36° a crank turn, clockwise seen from above when adding; (3) on its brass centre (Ø45) an outer circle of black 0–9 for + and an inner circle of red 0–9 for −; (4) the pin sits in a ring hole and points at the numbers; it stops the crank as now (so *Run to stop* keeps working), with "marker only" ready as a parameter for Badur's answer; (5) the *Magna Rota* view becomes a *Counter* view. Order: the working counter first (with clicking a hole to set the pin), then the Magna Rota's plates.
- **2026-11-24** **Correction: the counter can both mark and stop.** I had said that fixed numbers (Badur's text) and a pin that stops the crank (our model, after Leupold) could not go together. They can: the photo of the Dresden replica, `Die_Rechenmaschine_von_Leibniz_(Nachbau)_2001_06.jpg` (Commons), shows the whole counter: an outer circle 0–9 engraved on the fixed plate, running anticlockwise from 0 at the top; the steel ring with 10 holes; an inner circle 0–9 on the brass centre, running clockwise; **a steel bracket at the top, screwed to the plate and reaching over the ring between 0 and 9: a fixed stop**; and a parking place for the pin at the bottom. The pin set opposite digit *m* reaches the stop after *m* turns, counting down: Leupold's procedure, on the counter. Badur's procedure (pin at 0, turn until it points at the digit) uses the same hardware without the stop. The two circles run opposite ways, one for each direction (the book: outer +, inner −). Brandt's CAD figure shows one circle and no bracket, perhaps simplified; the replica agrees with the book. No photo shows numbers on the original's Magna Rota.
- **2026-11-24** **Counter plan, corrected:** built like the replica photo: outer circle fixed on the plate, ring with 10 holes turning 36° a crank turn, inner circle on the brass centre, the stop bracket at the top between 0 and 9, a pin parking place at the bottom. Behaviour unchanged (pin in hole *m*, stopped after *m* turns, *Run to stop*). Then the Magna Rota becomes the owner's three plates, turning with the crank. Nothing here waits for Badur.
- **2026-11-24** **Done: the revolution counter and the Magna Rota.**
  - *Counter* (on the carriage's lower right top): `pm.counter_plate` (fixed: the black outer circle, anticlockwise from 0 at the stop; the red inner circle on the brass centre, clockwise; the spindle; the stop: a post and a finger over the ring), `pm.counter_ring` (steel, Ø69.5 / Ø45 with 10 real holes, turning 36° a crank turn about the vertical, clockwise seen from above when adding), `pm.counter_pin` (in its hole) and `pm.counter_pin_park` (the pin put away, shown when it is out). The pin set at black *m* rests on the stop after *m* turns; turned back, 9 − *m* turns are clear and the next meets the finger's far face, as before. Clicking a hole (or its black digit) sets the pin; clicking the pin takes it out.
  - *Magna Rota*: the owner's three plates (satin brass in front, two steel plates behind), Ø116.5, fixed to the crank and turning with it; clicking it turns the crank. Curves flattened to 0.08 mm: the model grows from 154k to 173k triangles.
  - The simulation is unchanged; only the pose moved (`counterRing`, and `magnaRota` now the crank's angle). New *Counter* view (straight from the front and above: a sideways tilt turns the picture and a round dial then fills less of it). The lessons' counting steps use it; the tutorial's crank step keeps the *Magna Rota* view. Params 0.5.0. All tests and Blender's clearance sweep pass.
  - *Known, for the text round:* the lessons on multiplying and dividing and the tutorial still speak of "the Magna Rota's pin", its holes and its black and red numbers; hovering the counter says "Magna Rota" (the names reuse the old keys); dividing with the pin out shows no number on the model (on the original one sets the pin at 0 to count: the lesson's "red number" step needs rewriting); the dev page's examples say "Magna Rota"; the README's pictures show the old disc.
