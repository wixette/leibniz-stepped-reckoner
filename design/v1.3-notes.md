- **2026-11-24** **Correction: the counter can both mark and stop.** I had said that fixed numbers (Badur's text) and a pin that stops the crank (our model, after Leupold) could not go together. They can: the photo of the Dresden replica, `Die_Rechenmaschine_von_Leibniz_(Nachbau)_2001_06.jpg` (Commons), shows the whole counter: an outer circle 0–9 engraved on the fixed plate, running anticlockwise from 0 at the top; the steel ring with 10 holes; an inner circle 0–9 on the brass centre, running clockwise; **a steel bracket at the top, screwed to the plate and reaching over the ring between 0 and 9: a fixed stop**; and a parking place for the pin at the bottom. The pin set opposite digit *m* reaches the stop after *m* turns, counting down: Leupold's procedure, on the counter. Badur's procedure (pin at 0, turn until it points at the digit) uses the same hardware without the stop. The two circles run opposite ways, one for each direction (the book: outer +, inner −). Brandt's CAD figure shows one circle and no bracket, perhaps simplified; the replica agrees with the book. No photo shows numbers on the original's Magna Rota.

- **2026-11-24** **How the crank drives the machine: a hypothesis, to be checked against every source before modelling.** Neither the drums nor the counter are connected to the crank in the model: the crank and the Magna Rota turn, the drums turn in the simulation, and the back row of drive gears (Lehmann: drum and lever gears alternating) has nothing driving it. From Brandt's CAD figure (the owner's reading) and our sources:
  1. three large gears behind the Magna Rota (Lehmann's A, B, C: 61 teeth, m 1.5, 91.5 between centres; Lange 1958 pencils 91.38), the middle one on the crank's shaft;
  2. 91.5 is 2.5 digit places (91.25): with the crank half-way between two drums, the outer gears sit on two drum shafts (for example drums 1 and 6), turning them 1:1;
  3. the back row carries the motion to all 8 drums and 8 carry levers (16 gears);
  4. Burkhardt (1897) counts 17 crown wheels in the drive: the 17th would be an extra shaft beside the rightmost drum, whose front end carries a single tooth that steps a steel star wheel (axis front to back) once a turn; the star wheel turns a flat brass gear, which turns the counter's ring (the book: "um einen Schritt weitergeschaltet").
  Open: the crank's position (ours, 3.75 places from the units drum, is a photo estimate; the hypothesis needs 3.5), the counter gears' tooth counts (the ratio must give a tenth of a turn), the counter's direction. **Next (step 1): re-read all sources to confirm or correct this before any modelling.**
- **2026-11-24** **Step 1 done: how the crank drives the machine, checked against every source.** (Badur's drawings are private: only facts here.)
  - **The drive row has 17 gears, half a place apart** (high confidence). Badur's carriage frame (sheet 9, "Schlittenrahmen Pos. 2", 333 long) lists holes 1–17 at X = 14.75 + 18.25 *k*: the even holes (33.0 … 288.5, every 36.5, Y 12.5) are the 8 drum shafts, the odd ones (14.75 … 270.25, Y 17.4) the 8 carry levers' shafts half a place to the left of each drum, and **hole 17 (306.75, Y 17.4) a 17th shaft half a place to the right of the units drum**. The two heights differ by 4.9, Lehmann's lever-above-drum offset (NL 183/466 PLAN, Bild 1). Burkhardt (1897) counted "17 Kronräder" in the drive; Lehmann's schematic (466 PLAN p. 2) draws the row Z1, Z2 … alternating drum and lever gears.
  - **The row is at the front, and the drums' gears slide along long gears** (high confidence). Badur's Abb. II 9 (WALSDORF2014): at the front of each digit a long "drive gear" (*Antriebszahnrad*, u) on the shaft that runs back to the Zweihorn (t), meshing with a narrower gear on the drum's shaft beside the setting rack (v); Burkhardt's "Antriebscylinder": drums and drive cylinders too short, so the gears on the drums' axes came out of mesh at a full shift from 9 to 0. Lange (1958, 466 PLAN p. 9) also draws meshing gears in the carriage's front part. Our model, after Lehmann's replica, has the row at the back and fixed gears on square drum shafts.
  - **The crank is on drum 4's axis** (high confidence): Badur's plan view (assembly drawing) puts the crank's handle on drum 4 counted from the units drum as 0 (the fifth drum; checked again 2026-11-24: the crank lines up with the fifth setting-shaft column from the right, four places left of the units, the 64 mm dimension starting half a place right of the units column); his carriage frame has one large bore, "Lagerbuchse B" (bearing bush B), measured at about 145 mm from the origin, drum 4 being at 142.5. Ours is at 3.75 places (a photo estimate).
  - **Three large gears: B on drum 4's axis, A and C on the carry levers' shafts 2.5 places either side** (high confidence): B (Y 12.5) to a lever shaft 5 half-places away (Y 17.4) is √(91.25² + 4.9²) = **91.38**, exactly the distance Lange pencilled between B and C on the original (466 PLAN p. 9); his B has a square bore (a square shaft). So the crank turns drum 4 directly and levers 1 and 6 (counted from the units), feeding the row at three points. Their tooth counts on the original are not known (Lehmann's replica: 61, m 1.5, all three; Lange drew 65, 63, 60 teeth; in Brandt's figure the middle one looks smaller).
  - **The 17th shaft drives the counter** (high confidence for the path, medium for its parts): Badur's plan view dimensions the counter's centre **64 mm** from that shaft (so 82.25 right of the units drum; ours, after Lehmann's replica, 96.4); between them he draws a gear lying flat under the counter; Brandt's figure shows a steel gear standing upright beside a small lever, and a brass gear lying flat under the ring. Badur's sheet 8 (private) names the counter's parts: **Pos. 38 "Anschlagscheibe"** (stop disc: the steel ring Ø69.5 / Ø45 with its holes; the name confirms that the pin stops the crank against it), Pos. 39 a cross-bar, **Pos. 40 "Scheibe"** (brass, Ø45, digits 0–9 punched on a Ø42 circle), Pos. 41 its axle pin, **Zahnrad J** (Pos. 42, steel, 15 teeth, m 2.25), **Teilrad L** (Pos. 44, brass, an indexing wheel of 10 teeth at 36°, rack profile m 2.25: straight flanks, which suits meshing at right angles with J), and Zahnrad K (Pos. 43, steel, 15 teeth, m 3; read as 13 at first, corrected 2026-11-25) and **Zahnrad M** (Pos. 45, brass, 10 teeth, m 3, "centre distance with Rad N"), whose roles are not clear. A likely train: once a turn the 17th shaft pushes J on by one tooth; J turns L one tooth, 36°; L turns the ring. The single tooth, the detent, K, M and N, the ratio's details and the direction are not confirmed.
  - **Not available:** Badur's sheet 4 is missing from what he sent; his lettered gears run B, G, H, J, K, L, M, N, so A, C, D, E and F (likely the large gears and the drive row) are probably on it.

- **2026-11-24** **Follow-up on the drive row: two more sources, and what follows from the gears turning in opposite directions.**
  - **Lange's cut-away shows the row on the original** (NL 183/466 PLAN p. 9, "gezeichnet nach dem Original in der Landesbibliothek Hannover", March 1958). In the carriage's front trough, beside the setting racks, a window is cut into one of the short walls between the racks. Behind it, at least two upright gears with their axes front to back mesh side by side across the machine; a third shows just to the right. Long ribbed cylinders lie in the same trough, parallel to the racks (most likely the long drive gears u), and each setting dial's upright spindle carries a pinion on a rack (v) beside them. This is a fourth independent source, after Badur's measurements, Abb. II 9 and Burkhardt, that the row stands at the front in one upright plane. It is an exploded artist's view, not to scale: it does not show bearings, the count of 17, the two heights, or which gear sits on a drum's shaft and which on a lever's.
  - **Lehmann's replica photo** (Commons, "Die Rechenmaschine von Leibniz (Nachbau) 2001 03", Technische Sammlungen Dresden): three spoked gears lie in a row on the front brass plate, the middle one on the crank's axis. It confirms the layout of A, B, C at the crank end, not the shafts A and C sit on; the drive row is not visible.
  - **Directions:** neighbouring gears in the row turn opposite ways, so all drums turn one way and all lever shafts the other. B (on drum 4) turns opposite to A and C, which therefore sit on lever shafts, as the 91.38 distance says. Badur's Abb. II 9 agrees: the arrows on the Zweihorn (t) and the drum (m) point opposite ways.
  - **A, B and C must have equal teeth:** the crank is on drum 4, so a drum turns once per crank turn; a lever shaft must also turn exactly once, or its Zweihorn would drift out of phase. So z_A = z_B = z_C (Lehmann's 61, m 1.5). Lange's 65 / 63 / 60 are counts of his drawn teeth (as §9 of notes-nl183 says), and the tooth count no longer needs to be asked of Badur.
  - **Plan agreed (owner, 2026-11-24): model it now**: crank and big gears A, B, C on drum 4 and lever shafts 1 and 6; the counter at 82.25; the 17-gear front row with long drive gears and sliding drum gears; the 17th shaft.   - **Abb.
