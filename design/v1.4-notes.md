## Asked of Klaus Badur (2026-11-27; answered 2026-11-28, see the log)

The owner thanked him for his replies of 25 November (the crank's direction, the Magna Rota, the revolution counter; his plan view of 21 November) and told him, with the Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Bibliothek in copy, what 1.3.0 built from them, inviting both to try it. Asked:

1. How high do the pentagons' top edges stand above the result discs, or how long are the intermediate shafts above the register?
2. Is the original's register open from above, without a cover plate?
3. From which angle of the carry store do the original's detent springs complete a carry?

## Log

- **2026-11-27** v1.3.0 released (PR #6, merge commit `3c3e458`, tag and GitHub release `v1.3.0`; the site deployed). The branch `dev-v1.3.0` was deleted, locally and on GitHub, and `dev-v1.4.0` opened from `main`. The next release's number is provisional: it depends on what Badur's answers change.
- **2026-11-28** **Klaus Badur's reply** (one mail, the GWLB in copy; with a photo of his replica, private: facts only). He finds the simulator a very good way to understand the machine and will send remarks when he has them.
  - **The lead screw** that shifts the carriage lies, on the original, **in the middle between the base's two guide rails** (his assembly drawing shows it). Its thread is a **cut (filed) groove**.
  - **The carriage's detent:** the front guide rail has **8 detent grooves** that fix the carriage's places exactly. The **lever with the detent pin is sprung, fixed to the carriage**; the operator **releases it by hand before shifting**.
  - **Answers to the three questions:**
    1. The pentagon discs are fixed to the **rear ends of the register's intermediate shafts**, 40 across (as measured before). The shafts are 36.5 apart, so they are **of different lengths** to avoid collisions as the discs turn: the discs lie **alternately behind and in front of the base frame's bar** ("Grundrahmen-Leiste"). No height given.
    2. **The original has no cover over the register.** He supposes one was there originally, to keep the machine's working from onlookers; his replica has one.
    3. The detent springs complete a carry over **only a few degrees**; he has no exact figure.
  - **Lehmann's replicas** (with their changed Zweihorn angles) do **not** finish the carries of all 8 places within 360° of the crank: they need about **30° more**, up to just before the stepped drum takes hold of the receiving gear again. He tried it on Lehmann's replica at the Berlin Academy of Sciences, which he had for repair.
  - **His replica is built as the original** (with Wolfgang Rottstedt, in Rottstedt's workshop), to find out why the original supposedly cannot work. On it he ran a test calculation after a protocol by Leibniz's assistant **R. C. Wagner of 1706**: two 8-digit numbers multiplied to a 16-digit result. It went **without having to finish carries in between with 0 on the knobs**. The protocol also shows that the machine could calculate correctly at times.
  - **His photo** (his replica, private): an oak case with a hinged lid; an engraved brass cover over the register with the pentagons standing above it at the back; turned feet on wooden blocks inside the case; the end crank outside the left wall; the lead screw in a steel channel behind the front rail, with the detent's marks along the rail.
- **2026-11-28** **Steps 1–3 of the plan done** (owner: work through the improvements that need no more from Badur, then release 1.4.0 and write to him): the References text on Lehmann's carry ("a little more than one turn, about 30° more"), ten languages; a test that the original's carry multiplies 8 × 8 digits with no idle turns in between (Wagner's protocol, as Badur ran it); the carriage's detent as Badur describes it, grooves across the front rail and a sprung catch lifted around each shift (collision sweep clear).
  - **Our Original carry against the Wagner test:** in 300 random 8 × 8-digit products, no idle turns are needed in between (carries waiting mid-way are finished by the next turns, before or after the digit transfer, as his book says); 30 % end with 1–4 carries still waiting, which 1–2 idle turns finish; 3 % leave a carry in a store the carriage has already passed (units first, it moves left), which only a shift back over it finishes. Whether his run needed idle turns at the end is to ask (with the protocol's two numbers, to run it here).

The ripple set's planes (behind the drums, at the register's height) are not touched.

  - **Adjusted after the owner's check (2026-11-28):** the pits' shape kept, and the stand. The J is a band thin from above (1.0) and tall from the front (3.4, z 15.5–18.9; the stand's top lowered to 15.4 to make room), its front no further forward than the carriage's front panel (y −110.5), its hook's left side about level with the stand's tip (outer R 4.55); its head a small knob at the band's end, R 1.28 against the pit's 1.25, so that from above it sits almost wholly in the pit (0.92 deep).
