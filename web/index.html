Leibniz's calculating machine

Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz's stepped reckoner (c. 1693–1716) was the first calculating machine for all four operations of arithmetic. Its stepped drum was used in calculators for 300 years. Set a number on its knobs, turn the crank, and watch it add, carry, multiply and divide, from the inside.

These pages show the mechanism step by step, in English. They were used to build and check the model.

  • One digit: set a digit, turn the crank, watch the stepped drum, the result wheel and the carry store.
  • Three digits and the carry: 999 + 1 in ripple and historical carry modes, with the Zweihorn levers, carry stores and the drive chain.
  • The full machine: 16-digit register, 8-digit carriage with shifting, and the Magna Rota. Worked multiplication and division, and both readings of the carry.
